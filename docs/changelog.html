Changelog

2026-10-25

The Blueprint tab can now use stack inserters: tick "Allow stack inserters" (it starts ticked when your research is limited and includes the Stack inserter technology). Measured in the game, a stack inserter moves about three times what a fast inserter does from a belt, so stages need fewer inserters; layouts with them were tested at their target rates. The bulk inserter was measured too and moves exactly what a fast inserter does until its capacity research is done, so it is not offered. The estimates of what each inserter moves are conservative: the game did about a quarter more than counted. A line blueprint now says when a part has more machines than the calculator counts (for example 3 assembling machines for 2 inserters a second): the inserters beside a machine cannot always move its ingredients fast enough to keep fewer busy, and each part is sized a little over the rate. When a model chooses the layout, any machines beyond what its own inserters need are cut back, and a working layout with more machines than the calculator's own search needs is sent back once with how the search gets by with fewer. Lines can now include a stage which takes a fluid, such as plastic from coal and petroleum gas. It has to be the outermost stage of a branch: its machines face the supply belt, their pipes tunnel under it to a vertical pipe beyond, and the supply belt comes in on a row above where the fluid connects. A row is also now only used in one branch of a line. Lines can now be laid out by a local model too (or Claude with your API key), the same way as columns and chains: choose the way to build above the Generate button. The model picks the machine counts, belts, inserters and poles of every stage and the final recipe, and is told why a layout is rejected until one works. Lines can now end in a recipe with three or four solid ingredients, such as inserters (iron plate, gears and circuits). Such a final recipe takes from a second shared belt beside the first, reached over it by long-handed inserters, and an ingredient brought in from outside arrives on a feeder. A stage in a branch may also take one more ingredient from outside: for example the circuit assemblers get iron plate on a feeder while the cable comes from the stage beyond. So iron and copper plates can now be built all the way to inserters. The Blueprint tab can now build whole lines from the raw inputs. When a recipe in the factory has the rows that make its ingredients (and what makes those) alongside it, the new Lines section offers one blueprint for all of it: for example copper furnaces, then cable assemblers, with iron furnaces on the other side, all feeding the electronic circuit assemblers, so only copper ore and iron ore have to be brought in. Each branch steps outward from a shared belt, one stage's output belt being the next stage's input belt, and the two ingredients keep to their own lanes of the shared belt. Branches are up to two stages deep, and, as for chains, the parts are the cheapest which carry the load. Chains can now be laid out by a local model too: choose "Ask a local model (Ollama)" for a chain as well as for a single column. The model is asked for the machine counts, belts, inserters and poles of both stages and the shared belt, and told why any layout is rejected until one works. The model box is now a list of the models your Ollama has installed (with a Refresh button), and falls back to typing a name if the list cannot be fetched. The prompts also now tell the model that machines cost far more than inserters, so it should upgrade an inserter before adding machines. The blueprint preview is now a picture instead of text characters: machines drawn with their icons and the recipe they make, belts showing direction and tier (yellow, red, blue, green), inserters as arrows in the direction items move, pipes and underground pipes, and poles joined by their wires. Markers show where to supply each ingredient and fluid and where the product comes out. You can zoom, show each pole's power coverage, hide the labels, and hover any part for its name. The text version is still there under "Text version". The Blueprint tab can now build chains. When one row of the factory makes an ingredient of another (for example copper cable for electronic circuits, or iron plate for circuits), it offers a single blueprint with both: the two machine groups share one belt, each filling its own lane, with the other ingredient brought in by a feeder belt at the top. The blueprint tells you where to supply each ingredient and where the product comes out.

2026-10-24

There is a new, experimental Blueprint tab. For each electric furnace or assembling machine row in the current factory (with up to two solid ingredients, and, for chemical plants, one or two fluids brought in by pipe) it can build a column of machines (belts, inserters, furnaces and poles) and give you a blueprint string for the game, either from the cheapest parts which carry the load or with a model choosing them: a local Ollama model (nothing leaves your machine), or Claude using your own API key. Every layout is checked for belt and inserter capacity, overlaps and power before a string is produced. Buildings which do not burn chemical fuel now use the right fuel. Biochambers consume nutrients, and captive biter spawners consume bioflux, instead of showing coal. The fuel is counted in the solution like any other ingredient. The recipe toggles in Settings now show names, are grouped the way the game groups items, and can be searched. Recycling recipes are collected in their own section. The 2.1.23 dataset also includes the technology tree: under Settings, Research, you can choose "Only what I have researched" and tick off technologies (or pick everything up to a given science pack) to lock the recipes you have not unlocked yet. Locked recipes are marked with the technology that unlocks them, and can still be turned on by hand. "Import from my game" takes the list of researched technologies from a console command, so the calculator can match your save exactly. If a factory still uses a locked recipe (because you turned it on by hand), the results are marked and list the technologies it needs.

2026-10-23

A Space Age dataset for 2.1.23 has been added. The 2.1 data format lets a recipe list several crafting categories, and a building can craft it if it supports any one of them; the calculator now understands this. Most recipes are unchanged from 2.0.55. Notable differences: molten iron and molten copper are now made by the iron ore melting and copper ore melting recipes, wood comes from tree seeds, and the asteroid chunk reprocessing and piercing shotgun shell recipes have new numbers.

I've made some improvements to the Space Age dataset. Fulgora and Gleba mostly work now, though I still need to teach it about how biochambers eat nutrients; please ignore anything saying they eat coal.

Space platforms and Aquilo still to come.

2024-10-26

I have added a very early, experimental dataset for Space Age to the calculator. Expect bugs, strange issues, missing data, broken URLs, and the like. Some known issues include, and are not limited to:
  • Recycling recipes are currently disabled, meaning calculations for Fulgora are not very useful.
  • I haven't implemented anything about spoilage yet, so calculations for Gleba are not very useful.
  • The calculator doesn't understand asteroids yet, so calculations for space platforms are not very useful.
  • I don't even know yet what's wrong with Aquilo.
  • All sorts of UI strangeness.
Nauvis and Vulcanus seem to work okay, though. Use the planet selector in the settings to give it a try.

In particular, I want to stress that any URLs you get out of the calculator while using the Spage Age data right now will not be guaranteed to keep working going forward. So don't go too crazy with bookmarks just yet.

2024-10-15

This represents a major update for the Factorio Calculator. It has been virtually rewritten from scratch, updating the nearly ten year old codebase to a more modern version of JavaScript, and paving the way to better supporting Factorio 2.0 and Space Age. Several things have changed along the way, and this document will walk through them.

Factory tab changes

The Factory tab has received a handful of subtle changes. The most obvious is the beacons column.
A new beacon UI.
The old behavior was by far the most common point of confusion with the Factorio Calculator. The new layout more closely matches the game: Each row now has two module slots for beacons, and the count is now the number of beacons. If you want to run the numbers on your 4, 8, or 12-beacon setup, you no longer need to remember to multiply by two.

A more subtle change has to do with the selection of modules. Selecting the first module in a row will now set the remainder of the modules in that row. (Likewise, selecting the module for the first slot in a beacon will set both slots.)

Change the first module to change all the modules.
This replaces the old "arrow" button. You can alter or remove the other modules in the row if you wish, but it has been my observation that setting all of a building's modules to the same thing is, by far, the most common operation, and so I have streamlined this operation. (If you only wish to set one module, you can always set one other than the first one.)

Between this and the default module options, all of the old arrow buttons have been rendered obsolete, and they have been removed.

This feature actually works now.
If you specify a number of buildings for an item which may be created with multiple recipes, there is a dropdown which allows you to select which recipe will be used when interpreting the building count. While this feature has been present in the calculator for a long time, it didn't really work like one might have expected. This has changed, and the calculator will now actually calculate how to make that number of buildings of that specific recipe.
The arrows now point the other way.
The item breakdowns (the per-item tables that pop up when you click the arrows on the left hand side) have received the minor change of reversing the order of the icons and the arrow. This places the numbers representing the rates of individual items next to the icons of the items they represent, which should make things more clear.
You can ignore this now.
The item ignoring feature has been improved as a side effect of the new solver (see below). Various items which could not previously be ignored no longer have this limitation.

Setting Changes

The Settings tab has undergone a visual overhaul, and several of the settings have changed.
Set the window title with this.
A Title setting has been added. This will change the window title, aiding in organization if you run the calculator in multiple tabs.
People have been asking for this for a while.
A secondary default module has been added. This is useful, for example, if you wish to set a primary default for a productivity module, and then use speed modules for anything which does not support productivity modules.
You can change the miner now.
The old "minimum assembler" and "furnace" settings have been combined into a single generalized "buildings" setting. Each row represents a different class of building. As a consequence, it is also now also possible to configure the miner you wish to use for ore.
This is much more flexible than before.
You can now toggle some individual recipes on and off. This replaces (and ought to work better than) the old oil and Kovarex settings. This ought to give much more fine-grained control over which solutions the calculator will generate.

Visualizer Changes

The visualizer UI has changed.
The visualizer UI has been generally overhauled. Visualizations may be panned and zoomed more easily, though the old behavior has been retained as an option if you find that more convenient.
The box & line diagram is less angular.
The visualizations themselves haven't changed much, but the look of the box-and-line diagrams has been slightly improved.

The old option to change the direction of the visualizations has been removed. The feature was always an afterthought, and the visualizations were always designed to look best in the orientations in which they are now given: Horizontal for the Sankey diagram, vertical for the box-and-line diagram.

Resources Tab

You can control the calculator's priorities.
This all-new tab comes along with a new solver in the guts of the calculator. It takes functionality which was previously hard-coded and hidden away and exposes it to the user to be configured as they wish. It defines the resource priority for different resources, controlling the solver's preferences when it has the option to pick one recipe over another.

(As an aside, crude oil and water default to a lower weight because fluids in Factorio generally operate on a scale that is an order of magnitude larger than ordinary items. Dividing their weights in this way makes the numbers work out in a more expected fashion.)

This is not terribly useful in vanilla Factorio, but it may be more important in Space Age, and it opens the door to supporting more complex mods in the future.
